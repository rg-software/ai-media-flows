# Audio: scenario index

Goal: audio as **content** - the consumer is a player's ears, not an engine's sprite/rig pipeline. The sibling question to `video/`'s ("a viewer's file, not an engine asset"): here it is a *listener's* file.

First model: **ACE-Step 1.5** (MIT, commercial-ready by its own card, trained on licensed/royalty-free/synthetic data). It is the licence-cleanest thing in this repo - cleaner than the 2D stack's `insightface`, the 3D scenario's Hunyuan licence, or the Retro Diffusion pixel-art models.

## Knobs

- **Simultaneous voices depend on how hard you ask (E10 -> E14).** A *duet* request alone comes back **traded, not together** (E10). But when both the style text and the lyric markers demand overlap - "a male and a female lead sing the SAME words at the SAME time ... never trading lines and never solo", with `[Bridge] One breath, two voices` in the lyric - the user's ears confirm **two voices at once** (E14). The prompt is the lever; the recipe's own texts are in `workflows/scratch/duet-{style,lyric}.txt`. **How much** overlap you get still varies by model and style (E14): ACE-Step turbo and XL SFT both have simultaneous sections, YuE "sort of", and MiniMax - the best word-coverage arm - trades instead, so coverage and simultaneity do not rank the same. Some of it is a **choir doubling in unison** rather than two harmony leads.
- **YuE 2 (the cover flow) is `cc-by-nc-4.0` - non-commercial.** It is the only *restyle* route we have (ACE-Step keeps a reference but cannot restyle it, E5), so it is worth the licence for research, but nothing it makes can ship. Its **duration is the model's own** - `YuE2GenerateMusic` returns a FLOAT that drives the latent, bounded by `max_duration` - **and the cap is not a target**: E22 measured a 30 s instrumental at cap 30 coming out 27.7 s with a 188-word lyric and 29.5 s with none. **Added lyrics need a source with a sung line**, since an instrumental's transcribed `Vocal` line is all rests and the added words were not sung (E22). Its two-stage `YuE2GenerateABC` -> `abc` route is optional (dropping the link prunes the stage and makes the run ~30% faster, E12b).
- **Which music model to use (E13/E23)?** ACE-Step 1.5 **turbo** stays the iteration default (the 8-step model is fast but **prompt-fragile**). The two song arms are kept because **E23 held the style fixed and they split by style, not by rank**: on a clear-lead style **XL SFT** (4B) sings the most of a lyric (173 of 188, 92% coverage) at ~50 s and ~19 GB, while **v1 3.5B** runs ~2x faster and 2.5x smaller and sings the **shouty styles XL whispers on** (`lyrics_strength` is its knob, and it works only in a window). Then: **MiniMax Music 3** sings 90 in ~18 min (44.1 kHz, 'MiniMax-Music3' display term) and **YuE 2** sings sparsely and is **non-commercial**.
- **Every model here generates at full scale (E11).** Five takes across two unrelated models and two unrelated graphs peaked at **0.0 dBFS**, so normalise before shipping anything - it is not one model's quirk.
- **`tags`** - what the music *is*: genre, mood, instrumentation, production, as prose (the template ships a paragraph of Neo-Soul tags). This is the prompt.
- **`lyrics`** - empty means instrumental; **size it to the duration** (E7): the model sings about **60%** of the words it is given, i.e. 1.5-3 words per second, so a 120 s track wants **250-350 words**; markers alone change nothing, but verse/chorus/bridge with a repeated chorus does; the template ships a complete demo song's lyrics, with `[Verse]` / `[Chorus]` / `[Intro - …]` section markers.
- **`duration`** (seconds, drives the latent) - **and it gates the vocals**: at 30 s the flow is a music-only generator even with a full lyric, while 120 s sings (E6), **`bpm`**, **`keyscale`**, **`timesignature`**, **`language`** (50+), **`seed`**.
- The sampler side: the template uses `cfg_scale` **2**, `temperature` **0.85**, `top_p` **0.9**, `min_p` 0, 8 steps (`KSampler` with `ModelSamplingAuraFlow`), and `generate_audio_codes` on.

## Lyric section markup - what `[Verse]` and `[Chorus]` actually are

**A convention the model learned, not a keyword list, and nothing in the pipeline parses it.** The node hands the lyric straight to the tokenizer (`clip.tokenize(tags, lyrics=lyrics, ...)` in the ACE-Step node module, `nodes_ace.py`, inside ComfyUI's core `comfy_extras`) - no vocabulary, no validation, and no error for an unknown name. The evidence is what the ten shipped ACE-Step templates actually write: **21 distinct bracketed tokens**, in two kinds.

| kind                                         | examples from the shipped templates                                                                                                                                  |
|----------------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **structural** - what shapes the arrangement | `[Intro]` `[Verse]` `[Verse 1]` `[Verse 2]` `[Pre-Chorus]` `[Chorus]` `[Post-Chorus]` `[Final Chorus]` `[Bridge]` `[Outro]`                                          |
| **free-form** - a hint, never validated      | `[Intro - Rhodes piano and brushed drums]`, `[Chorus - Pop Explosion]`, `[Verse 1 - Trap Rap]`, `[instrumental]`, `[break down]`, `[drum fill]`, `[chopped samples]` |

Use the standard names for structure and a dash plus a phrase for arrangement hints. An invented name is not an error - it may simply mean nothing - and **we cannot measure which**, because a section label changes the arrangement rather than the words, so neither the transcript nor the spectrogram settles it. That is what ears are for. (The v1 node exposes `lyrics_strength`, a weight on how hard the lyric is followed; v1.5 has no such knob and carries the structured parameters - `bpm`, `keyscale`, `timesignature`, `duration` - instead.)

## Composition, mixing and repair - built, and what it taught (E15-E19)

The 2D scenario composes rather than hoping one prompt carries three characters: mask, move, paste, inpaint. Audio has the same machinery, and most of it is already in core - checked on this build:

| job                                                                      | nodes / tools                                                                                                                                                                     |
|--------------------------------------------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **separate a take into stems**                                           | `audio-separator[cpu]` (E10's recipe; ephemeral through `uv run --with`) - verified in E15/E16 (**MDX** is the best of the three measured); **not yet shipped as a `scripts` tool**                                                            |
| **mix and blend**                                                        | `AudioMerge`, `AudioAdjustVolume`, `AudioEqualizer3Band`, `SplitAudioChannels` / `JoinAudioChannels`, `TrimAudioDuration` - an in-graph mixer                                     |
| **repair a region** (a mispronounced word, a deliberately deleted piece) | `VAEEncodeAudio` -> `SolidMask` + `MaskComposite` -> `SetAudioLatentNoiseMask` -> `KSampler` at partial denoise -> `VAEDecodeAudio` - the inpainting analogue, on an audio latent |
| **a target timbre**                                                      | `ReferenceTimbreAudio`; for new *words* on an existing tune, YuE's cover (S22) - but only when the source is already sung; an instrumental has no vocal line for them (E22)       |

**Match the route to the job - what this pipeline is actually for.** Source separation is an ill-posed inverse problem, so "generate a song, split it, recombine" will never be clean. Pick the route by goal:

| the goal                           | the route, in order of preference                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
|------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **an instrumental bed**            | ask for an instrumental directly (`lyrics` empty) - guaranteed clean. Only separate when the mix already exists, and then MDX (`UVR-MDX-NET-Voc_FT`) is the best of the three measured (BS-Roformer and Mel-Band Roformer both leave audible vocal), optionally followed by a **debleed/denoise** pass |
| **a reusable voice track**         | generate the voice *as a part* (a cappella prompt, or YuE's cover for a melody plus new words (sung source only, E22)); separation is a *salvage* tool, not a source - and a generated voice only sits on a bed taken from the same take (E17/E17b)                                                                                                                                   |
| **restyle / keep only the melody** | a **symbolic** reference - YuE's cover (S22) turns the reference into ABC, so the original's vocals cannot survive it (ACE-Step's m2m is latent/denoise-based and *retains* instead)                                                                                                                   |
| **repair a word or a region**      | the mask/inpaint route (`SolidMask` + `MaskComposite` + `SetAudioLatentNoiseMask`), not separation - E19                                                                                                                                                                                               |
| **two genuine voices**             | **deprioritised** - what the models deliver is a *unison choir*, which is a legitimate sound but not two leads (E14/E15), so it is only worth pushing if harmony itself is the goal                                                                                                                    |

**The outcome (E15-E19), and the structural finding.** The order was revised by the ears as it went: **E15** stems (the vocal stem is clean; a dense take's instrumental leaks) -> **E16** separation quality (**MDX is the best of the three measured** - ASR could not rank them, and must never be used to name stems) -> **E17** parts-first mixing (**PASS for the bed, MEH for the mix**: a *generated* vocal and a *generated* bed do not cohere - the same style and seed make both *reproducible*, not *compatible*) -> **E17b** the matched bed (separate the take, then comp - "better in sync") -> **E18/E19**: region repair, **SOLVED** by one 10-line custom node.

**What we do not do (accepted).** We cannot add **a new voice** - or new words - to an existing **instrumental melody**. E17 explains why the parts-first route cannot: the voice cannot come from generation, because the bed must be the take's own (E17b). The nearest routes are **S22** (restyle a *sung* source; YuE, non-commercial - E22) and **S24** (re-sing a span in the **take's own** voice - E19); voice conversion onto a given melody is out of scope for this repo.

**Repair verdict, corrected (E19): there IS a clean in-graph repair, and it is one line.** Everything below is the record of how we failed to find it - and the reason was **not** the audio latent: `SetLatentNoiseMask` unconditionally reshapes any mask to **4-D**, which is right for an image latent and wrong for a 3-D audio one, so `reshape_mask`'s `dims == 1` path (which needs a 3-D mask) was handed a 4-D one. **A 10-line custom node that stores the mask unchanged fixes it** (`SetAudioLatentNoiseMask`, `custom_nodes/audio_latent_mask/`), and then `LoadAudio -> VAEEncodeAudio -> SolidMask + MaskComposite (band) -> SetAudioLatentNoiseMask -> KSampler -> VAEDecodeAudio` does exactly what this topic wanted: on our working **MIT** ACE-Step core model, in **15.4 s**, it repaints a 6 s band and leaves the other 114 s **at the VAE round-trip level** (`mean|diff|` **0.0015** outside against **0.198** inside - a **127.7x** ratio, measured against an all-zero-mask control), and it **refills a deleted span at full level** (-14.77 dB against the original's -13.07 dB, where the pack's repaint managed -32.8 dB). So the journeys below are history, and the practical answer is now: **repaint the span** - with the standing caveat that a confined repaint is still a *different performance* of that span, so the join is a listening question (E18c), and the mask edges are hard (add `GrowMaskWithBlur` if a click shows up at the boundary).

**Repair verdict, superseded (E18-E18d):** there is no clean in-graph repair, and **E18d closed the last open door.** The mask route is blocked by the latent geometry; the latent splice rewrites the untouched audio; a one-word edit re-performs the whole take; a waveform comp preserves the surroundings exactly but the seams are prominent (E4's crossfade mechanism - two takes are uncorrelated in phase and timing). **And ACE-Step's own purpose-built repaint node does not rescue it either** - it is MIT, it runs, and it is the fastest music route measured here (15.6 s per 120 s take), but **it is not region-limited**: repainting 55-61 s of an *uncut* take rewrote everything outside it too (`max|diff| 1.235`, diverging from the first second), and repainting a *deleted* span returned a passage ~20 dB under its surroundings. Four independent routes, one answer: **the practical answer is re-render and take the new master**, or a comped seam you can hear. (E18d's by-product is a box lesson: that node pack had **never run here** - its submodule was cloned from the wrong GitHub repo.) **Discipline carried over:** every model here lands at **0.0 dBFS**, so a mix needs explicit normalisation; E4's join rule *inverts* for edits (a hard join was right for a *loop*, a crossfade is the tool for music edits); and a transcript is a **lower bound** on vocal presence, never a presence test.

## Traps (measured, not read)

- **The official template uses `PrimitiveNode`s** (frontend-only nodes that feed widget inputs). A UI->API converter must substitute their *value* into the consumer **and must collect them in a pre-pass** - a consumer can be visited before its primitive (nodes 98 and 99 here), and the resulting prompt otherwise links to a node that is not in it, which the server rejects outright. Fixed in `scripts/sweep_workflow.py` (E1).
- **A flac *file* md5 is not a determinism test (E13).** Two runs of ACE-Step XL SFT produced different file md5s but **sample-identical audio** (`max|diff| 0.000000` over 5,760,000 samples) - the container carries metadata that moves. Compare decoded samples (E2's bit-exactness test used file md5, which happened to work on that model).
- **Whisper needs 16 kHz, and anything else produces *confident* garbage, not an error (E13c - the third time a tool here has misled us).** `faster-whisper` resamples a **file path** through ffmpeg but **not a bare numpy array**, so handing it this scenario's 44.1/48 kHz arrays yields **hallucination loops** (`The The The The...`, `I'll talk to you`) - which read as *"the model didn't sing the lyric"*, i.e. as a result. It cost a whole ladder of false conclusions, including the `0 transcribed words` claims struck from E19/E19b/E18d/E18e. **Always resample first** (`soxr.resample(mono, sr, 16000)`) or pass a path - and when a transcript looks wrong, transcribe a **known-good take as a control** before believing it. This is why an `audio_check.py` in `scripts` (named, not yet written) should own the resampling.
- **There is no `soundfile` in either venv, and `torchaudio.load` now wants `torchcodec`** - so measurement uses `uv run --with soundfile` (the ephemeral-dependency pattern), with the STFT and stats in numpy.
- **For a loop, cut from the middle of a longer bed and join it hard** (E4): measured on an ambient bed, an 8 s segment cut from 4 s in wraps at **1.2x** the track's median adjacent sample step - seamless - while adding a 50 ms self-crossfade made the worst step near the wrap **31x** the median and put a visible broadband click in the spectrogram, because a crossfade *sums two uncorrelated passages* and their percussion lines up. So the usual advice is inverted here, and the thing to do is measure the wrap rather than assume.
- **`SaveAudioAdvanced` output prefixes are not rewritten by a sweep's `--out`** (only `SaveImage`/`SaveVideo` are), so an audio file lands under whatever prefix the workflow carries.
- **Crest factor and noise floor do not tell you whether it is music (E18e).** A low crest means *no isolated peaks*, not *sustained content*, and a high noise floor is equally consistent with a noise wash - so a take can read "dense, compressed, well-mixed" on every number and still be noise. This is now the **second** proxy in this scenario an ear had to correct (the first was transcription, E12c). The rules that follow: **a metric is a hint, and a spectrogram read by a person beats a number** (E1's rule restated); and when a statistic and the ears disagree about *quality* rather than about *words*, **the ears are the verdict and the statistic gets demoted to "interesting"**.
- **We cannot listen, but we can verify two of the three things listening would tell us** (E3): (1) **is it music** - a **spectrogram**, the audio analogue of this repo's "look at the image": harmonics are horizontal lines, a beat is regular vertical transients, noise is a featureless wash, and sections are visible as texture changes; (2) **are the words right** - **transcription** (`faster-whisper`), which on the demo song put **83%** of the sung words back into the input lyrics and recovered **74%** of them. Taste is what remains out of scope, and it is the only thing that is. **(Corrected in E12c: transcription is a lower bound on vocal presence, not a presence test - the user's ears hear a vocal in YuE takes where Whisper read 0-19 words of a dense or shouted mix, so a transcript of "nothing" means unreadable, not absent.)**
