# PIPELINES: the capability map

**The problem this file solves.** `2d/` and `3d/` say *what you are making*, but our flows are increasingly *operations* that cut across that: `seamless-texture` serves 2D backgrounds **and** 3D materials; `ipadapter-style` works on objects and on scenes; `pixel-art` is a stylisation, not a scenario. So scenario folders no longer answer "which tool for this job?".

**Where things live:** scenario folders keep *assets, experiments and scenario docs*. This file is the **capability index** — every flow, what operation it performs, on what kind of subject, and its status.

**Which file do I want?** Three complementary views, all sharing the taxonomy below:

- **have a job** ("I need a cutout", "I need PBR maps") -> **[`HOWTO.md`](HOWTO.md)** for the steps, knobs and traps, plus the "I want to…" table in [`README.md`](README.md) for which flow.
- **want the landscape** (what families exist, what's supported, what licence, what knobs) -> this file.
- **adding a flow** -> this file: give it a family, subject, domain, cost, licence and key knob.

## Axis 1: operation family (what transformation it performs)

|----|-----------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|

| 2  | **guide**       | generation constrained by structure **or reference**                    | `sdxl-controlnet-canny`, `controlnet-union`, `ipadapter-style`, `regional-prompting`, `kontext-character`, `instantid-character`, `pulid-identity` |

Read the family as the answer to "what does this flow *do to* an asset?". A harness such as the sweep runner does nothing to an asset, so it is `tooling` rather than pretending to be an operation. Doc-maintenance tooling (`lint_docs.py`, `mdtable.py`, `add_workflow_notes.py`) acts on the docs rather than on assets, so it is documented for contributors in `AGENTS.md` and deliberately has no row here.

## Axis 2: subject and domain (what it is applied to)

- **subject**: `object` (single prop/character) · `scene` (composite picture) · `material` (flat surface/texture) · `character` (a person-like subject, used by the animation rows) · `cue` (a piece of music or an audio cue) · `clip` (recorded or loopable audio)
- **domain**: `image` (2D pixels) · `texture` (2D, tiling/PBR authoring) · `3D model` (mesh) · `video` (a clip a viewer plays) · `animation` (a rig or sprite sheet an engine consumes)

These are *attributes*, not folders — one flow can serve several. Record them per flow in the index.

**Extending to new modalities** (`film` is not the split here: `video`, `animation` and `audio` are all scenario folders now, and `audio` arrived first as *new domain values on the existing families* - `generate / scene / audio` - which is exactly what this paragraph asked for). Operation families are deliberately **modality-agnostic**: `generate` means *prompt → brand-new artefact*, whether that artefact is an image, a clip or a sound. So a new modality should arrive as **new `domain` values** (`animation`, `audio`) on the *existing* families — not as new families. Text-to-music would be `generate / scene / audio`, not a family called "compose".

The `subject` vocabulary (`object | scene | material`) was image-flavoured, and **audio is what forced the decision**: `cue` for a piece of music, `clip` for recorded or loopable audio, with `character` promoted to the list because the animation rows were already using it. `video` and `animation` need no companions beyond those.

### Bridge flows (`from->to`)

Some flows do not produce *within* one domain, they **move data between domains**. Rather than inventing ad-hoc strings, those are written in the domain column in an explicit, defined form:

| written             | means                                                       | flows                                                                                            |
|---------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| `texture->3D model` | 2D material authoring becomes data a 3D engine/DCC consumes | `scripts/marigold_pbr.py`, `scripts/make_gltf_material.py`, `2d/workflows/pbr-from-texture.json` |

So a `domain` cell is either one of the three domains above, or an explicit `A->B` bridge. The per-flow **notes** say what it consumes and what it emits.

## Motion and animation

Scoped 2026-09-25, before any flow work, because the space splits sharply and most of it is not ours. It is now **two scenarios**, split by who consumes the artefact: **`animation/`** (a sprite sheet or a rig - an engine asset) and **`video/`** (a video file - a viewer's asset). Every cell below is built and passing; what stays open is generating motion we do not have.

**Form** (what the artefact is) vs **source** (where the motion comes from):

| form | output | consumer | status |
|---|---|---|---|
| **parametric (3D)** | rig + skeletal clips | engine | **works with a recipe** - `animation/workflows/sam3d-motion.json` recovers motion from footage and exports an animated skeletal GLB (E4/E8), gated by `scripts/motion_quality.py`; the skin is Meta's MHR, so retargeting onto our rig stays engine-side, and fast clips keep one residual pop (E6). The *rigger* half works (`3d/workflows/unirig-humanoid-workflow.json`) |
| **sprite (2D)** | frame-indexed sheet / flipbook | engine | **works** - `2d/workflows/pose-extract.json` -> `2d/workflows/pose-skeleton.json` -> `scripts/atlas.py` |
| **video** | a video file | a viewer, not an engine | **works** - `video/workflows/wan-animate-motion.json` (E1). This is the `video/` scenario: the artefact decides the folder, and a viewer's file is not an engine's |

| motion source | tool | cost | status |
|---|---|---|---|
| **an existing clip library** (Mixamo etc.) | retargeting | free | **not ours - see below** |
| **video reference** | `video/workflows/wan-animate-motion.json` (Wan Animate 2) | Wan Animate 2 int8, 24 GB one-time | **PASS** (E1/E2) - 91 s, 20.8 GB peak at 480x848/33 frames |
| **keyframes / pose sequence** | skeletal keyframing, pose-to-motion | tooling we do not have | open |
| **text prompt** | video models, then retarget | large weights, and a two-stage problem | open |

**Pruned branch - retargeting an existing clip is engine work.** Unity's Humanoid avatar system maps an FBX clip onto any humanoid rig, ours included: `unirig-humanoid` already ships `fbx_preset = "mixamo"`, so the skeleton is Mixamo-compatible by construction. A ComfyUI retarget flow would be a slower, non-interactive copy of a solved problem - a 90 s run with no timeline and no playback, producing a baked FBX where the engine gives a re-targetable clip. UniRig's own `apply_animation` demo (rig + `Breakdance.fbx`) is exactly this, so UniRig's animation half drops out: it is a **rigger** that ships a retarget demo. **What is left of animation is generating motion we do not have**, which is where the weights decision is unavoidable.

A second flow here is a **finish** rather than a motion source: `video/workflows/frame-interpolate.json` (S18) synthesises in-between frames with RIFE, which either smooths a clip at its own speed or makes real slow motion - and it is the cheapest flow in the repo (15-25 s for a 5 s 720x1280 clip).

**Reachability today:** all three forms are built. Rigging works (UniRig, weights present), **video-reference motion is measured and passing** (E1), and the **parametric (3D)** cell is a working recipe (E4). Note the video-reference flow emits a *video* - a viewer's artefact, not a rig - which is why it sits in `video/` and not here.

### What the 3090 can afford (scoped, then measured 2026-09-25)

The nodes were already on the box (`WanAnimateToVideo`, `WanAnimate2ToVideo`, `WanMoveTrackToVideo`, `WanTrackToVideo`, `WanVaceToVideo`, `CreateVideo`/`SaveVideo`), so video-reference motion needed **weights only, no install**. The route was scoped from published figures first and then measured - and the measurement corrected the literature, which is why both are kept below.

| configuration | VRAM (predicted) | note |
|---|---|---|
| Wan 2.2 14B **fp8**, T5 on GPU | ~22-26 GB (720p) | at or over the 24 GB line |
| Wan 2.2 14B **fp8**, T5 offload | ~14-16 GB | workable in principle |
| Wan 2.2 14B **GGUF Q5**, T5 offload | ~8-10 GB (720p) | the recommendation - and **low by ~2x** |
| Wan 2.2 14B **GGUF Q4**, T5 offload | ~7-9 GB | minimum viable |

**Measured (E1):** 480x848, 33 frames → **91 s, peak 20 844 MiB VRAM**. So the predicted ~8-10 GB was optimistic by about 2x: ComfyUI kept the umt5 encoder resident instead of offloading it, and the animate node's own overhead is real. The clip fits, with only ~3 GB to spare.

Two findings that survived the scoping and are now measured:

- **fp8 is emulated on Ampere, and partly rejected.** The 3090 raises `type fp8e4nv not supported in this architecture` (it wants `e5m2`) - the same Ampere limitation `comfyui/hardware.md` records for Qwen. That pushed us to a GGUF quant, which turned out to be **wrong for a quite different reason** (E2): a GGUF drops the `config` metadata that names the model type, so the animate model loaded as plain image-to-video and silently ignored the driving video. The official **int8** `wan_animate_2_int8_convrot.safetensors` is the right choice - int8, not fp8, so the Ampere objection does not apply.
- **Cost grows with frame count** in the temporal-attention phase, so the ~3 GB of headroom is what bounds `length` in a single pass; a longer clip needs the upstream `ContextWindowsManual` chunking rather than a bigger card budget.

**Cost was 23.9 GB of download** (diffusion model 16.7 GB int8 + umt5 6.3 + clip_vision 1.2 + lightx2v LoRA 0.7 + VAE 0.24). Wan Animate 2 is **Apache-2.0**, so unlike the Flux/Kontext tier this route is licence-clean to ship from.
