| E6  | 2026-09-27 | motion quality (jitter/breaks) | recovered-motion quality     | `street_dance_drive.mp4` (fast dance) against the near-static E4 clip                       | `SAM3DBody_Smooth.rotation_threshold_degrees` {15 (the template's), 89, 0} x method {gaussian, savgol} x strength {1, 0}, measured with `scripts/motion_quality.py`                       | GLBs `output/3d/dance_{raw,smooth,savgol,thr89,thr0}_00001.glb`; sheets `output/video/{dance_break_check,smooth_compare}.png`              | **MEH** (recipe found; fast motion still breaks)     | The user's requirement was that recovered motion be *sensible, not jittery, not broken* - so this was tested on fast motion, which the E4 clip never stressed. **Moderate motion is clean** (E4: no breaks, jerk median 2.1). **Fast motion breaks**, and visibly: 8 joints carry single-frame steps over 45 deg (up to **174**), which the sheet shows as the mesh's orientation snapping. **No smoothing setting removes them**, and the reason is in the knob's own tooltip: `rotation_threshold_degrees` *disables* smoothing above a root rotation rate 'to preserve fast spins', and the template ships **15** - below the schema default of 30 - so smoothing switches itself off exactly when the motion gets fast. Raising it is a real lever: jerk median **16.60 -> 6.84 -> 2.75** (and breaks 8 -> 5 -> 4) for thresholds 15 -> 89 -> 0, so the template's value is the worst of the three tested. `method` (gaussian vs savgol) and `strength` change nothing while the threshold keeps disabling it. **Verdict: use it for moderate motion, set the threshold to 0, and gate every clip with `motion_quality.py`** - a fast or spinning clip can still flip (two ~173 deg pops remained at the hardest frames). One further knob: `camera_translation` is `off` in the template, which keeps the root at its bind position, so the render does not sit on the performer (`centered`/`absolute` bake the camera translation in) - relevant to root motion for an engine. |
| E7  | 2026-09-27 | does slowing the source help?  | recovered-motion quality     | `dance_2x.mp4` - each frame of the dance clip duplicated, so 121 frames become 242          | `SAM3DBody_Smooth` threshold 0 (the E6 recommendation), whole clip; measured with `scripts/motion_quality.py`                                                                             | `output/3d/dance_2x_00001.glb`                                                                                                             | **MEH** (partial - it helps jitter, not wrong poses) | The hypothesis was that a user can always slow the source to get a slow animation, so fast motion is not our problem. Measured against the same setting on the original clip: **breaks 4 -> 2** and bandwidth 1.03 -> 0.98 Hz. So it does help - more than expected, and for a specific reason worth knowing: repeating each pose gives the temporal smoother more consistent samples to average against, so an isolated pop is more easily absorbed. **But duplication cannot repair a wrong pose** - the largest flip survived almost intact (162 deg) and now simply lasts twice as long. Duplication is also *not* the same as shooting at a higher frame rate: duplication repeats poses, whereas a higher fps gives genuinely new intermediate ones, which is what would actually reduce per-frame motion. Conclusion: slowing a source is a reasonable mitigation for jitter and isolated pops, not a fix for a flip, and the break gate stays the arbiter.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
